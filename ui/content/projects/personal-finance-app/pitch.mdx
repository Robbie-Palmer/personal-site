# A financial planner for people who still use spreadsheets

Personal Finance App records balances, ownership, currencies, debts, and forecasts in one model.

<PitchNotes>
  The working title is Asset Tracker. The target user currently chooses between budgeting apps that lack long-term
  planning, advisor software that costs thousands per year, and a spreadsheet they must maintain themselves.

  \[Sources]

  * [Personal Finance App overview](/projects/personal-finance-app?tab=overview)
  * [Problem statement](/projects/personal-finance-app?tab=overview#problem)
    \[/Sources]
</PitchNotes>

## The first user's finances span property, shares, and currencies

* A home owned with a partner, unequal deposits, and a mortgage rate about to reset
* Old employer stock priced in dollars, sold into sterling, then invested in US-heavy funds
* Closed accounts that still matter to the historical picture
* Monthly spending inferred from cash flow, without categorising every coffee

<PitchStep>
  Each case needs its own formulas, currency assumptions, and history. The spreadsheet owner maintains all of it.
</PitchStep>

<PitchNotes>
  These examples come from the finances the product needs to model first. A spreadsheet can calculate the
  relationships, but its owner must design, test, document, and repair every formula.

## Start in the UK

* 15.16 million active Open Banking users by July 2025
* ISA, SIPP, CGT, dividend allowance, and Scottish income-tax rules
* GBP defaults with native support for accounts in other currencies
* The same account and entry model can support tax rules for Canada, Australia, and Europe

<PitchNotes>
  Most competing products start with US tax rules. The UK has mature Open Banking infrastructure and country-specific
  rules that generic international presets handle poorly. Manual entry keeps the alpha independent of aggregation
  providers. A later release can add bank feeds to the same entry workflow.

  \[Sources]

  * [UK structural advantage](/projects/personal-finance-app?tab=overview#uk-structural-advantage)
  * [Open Banking reaches 15 million users](https://www.openbanking.org.uk/news/open-banking-surges-to-15-million-uk-users-as-july-marks-record-adoption/)
    \[/Sources]
</PitchNotes>

## Validate with the founder, then add one group at a time

1. **Founder.** The app replaces the original spreadsheet.
2. **Partner.** Shared finances work without either person maintaining the model.
3. **Invitees.** Different households and countries expose bad assumptions.
4. **Public launch.** Invited users return and add more accounts without prompting.

<PitchNotes>
  Each group introduces new financial structures while the user base remains small enough for direct feedback. The
  first test is whether the founder can stop maintaining the spreadsheet. Partner and invitee behaviour then tests
  whether the model works beyond one person's finances.

## Measure accounts and history, not daily opens

<PitchColumns>
  <PitchColumn>
    ### Usage analytics

    Opens, streaks, and time in app.
  </PitchColumn>

  <PitchColumn>
    ### Evidence of product fit

    Accounts tracked, months of history, linked mortgages, recurring transfers, and unprompted return.
  </PitchColumn>
</PitchColumns>

<PitchNotes>
  A user may need the product only a few times each month. Retention should correlate with the amount of financial
  history and account structure stored in the product. Fifteen accounts, six months of balances, and a linked joint
  mortgage say more than a daily-open streak.

  \[Sources]

  * [What PMF looks like](/projects/personal-finance-app?tab=overview#what-pmf-looks-like)
    \[/Sources]
</PitchNotes>

## Charge new users after the free validation period

<PitchColumns>
  <PitchColumn>
    ### Before product-market fit

    Full functionality, manual entry, zero aggregation cost, and lifetime-free access for early testers.
  </PitchColumn>

  <PitchColumn>
    ### After product-market fit

    New users pay for advanced forecasting, tax modelling, and support.
  </PitchColumn>
</PitchColumns>

<PitchStep>
  Revenue comes from subscriptions. The product does not sell ads or take referral commissions.
</PitchStep>

<PitchNotes>
  Feedback and account depth matter more than revenue from the small validation cohort. Manual entry avoids bank-data
  provider fees. Invited testers keep lifetime-free access. After validation, new users pay for advanced forecasts,
  tax calculations, and support.

## The alpha succeeds when one user stops maintaining two systems

The app must carry one household's balances, ownership, currencies, debts, and history on its own.

<PitchNotes>

  has not replaced the current workflow. Funding and a public launch come after this test.
